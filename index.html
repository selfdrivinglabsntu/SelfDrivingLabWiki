Our purpose

Democratizing self-driving labs for accelerating scientific discovery

One of the major bottlenecks in scientific discovery—particularly in high-throughput experimentation—is the reliance on manual, repetitive laboratory workflows. While commercial laboratory automation systems exist, they are prohibitively expensive for small labs and startups. Heimdall harnesses 3D printing technology to make laboratory automation affordable and precise, reducing human error, improving reproducibility, and enabling researchers to spend more time innovating instead of performing repetitive tasks.

When self-driving labs become open access, imagination becomes reality.

Closed loop volume correction

Vision-Guided Precision

You set a target volume, and HEIMDALL closes the loop: a camera photographs the pipette's volume dial, a Gemini vision-language model (VLM) reads the current volume, and a stepper motor turns the dial to the correct target volume.

Vision powered by Gemini-2.5-flash · VLM
Precision

Every position, every volume, every move deliberate.

HEIMDALL moves on a repeatable coordinate system and targets standard or custom labware down to the individual well — the whole plate mapped, addressable, and logged.

Target acquired. Dispense sequence initiated.

Perfect coordinates

Driven by G-codes, lead screws and a GUI that makes configuring well plate coordinates effortless, Heimdall delivers accurate, repeatable positioning across every experiment.

Calibrated for precision

A linear-actuator piston for reliable, repeatable volumetric aspiration and dispensation.

Emulates human technique

Reverse pipetting, tip-dipping, and controlled tip agitation — the same moves a skilled hand makes.

0% logged

Every action written to CSV — ready to replay.

Open hardware & software

Heimdall is built on an open ecosystem. Its 3D-printable hardware designs, electronics, Marlin firmware, G-code workflows, and control software are openly available, enabling anyone to build, customize, and extend the platform while accelerating collaborative scientific innovation.
